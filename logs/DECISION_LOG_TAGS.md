# Recruiter Decision-Log Tag Taxonomy

Last updated: 2026-08-24
Status: Active — source of truth for the public decision log (see `docs/plans/2026-08-23-001-feat-recruiter-decision-log-plan.md`)

Use tags only when the underlying case provides concrete evidence for the capability. Tags describe demonstrated capabilities, not merely technologies or concepts mentioned in a case.

The public site fetches a generated copy of the tags below (current tags only, never the future bank) from `site/data/decision-log-tags.json` in the public-staging repo. That copy is regenerated by the weekly automation each time it publishes an entry — this file is the only one hand-edited.

## Language policy

The `tag` values are stable machine identifiers and remain in English so existing entries, filters, tests, and URLs do not break. The Swedish display labels below are the canonical recruiter-facing labels for the Swedish experience. The English `tag` value remains available as a fallback and for technical review.

When a tag is added, renamed, promoted from the future bank, or its meaning changes, update its Swedish label and `shows:` description in the same change. The public copy must carry both the stable identifier and the localized label. A public GitHub profile or recruiter-site content change must also trigger the same language-sync check; it must not update English content alone.

## Current / generally applicable tags

```yaml
- tag: product-taste
  shows: Ability to recognize what makes a product, feature, workflow, or solution useful, coherent, simple, and worth building.

- tag: product-judgment
  shows: Ability to make sound product decisions under uncertainty, including trade-offs, scope, sequencing, and what not to build.

- tag: business-acumen
  shows: Understanding of how operational, customer, technical, and commercial considerations connect to business outcomes.

- tag: commercial-awareness
  shows: Awareness of costs, incentives, market realities, resource constraints, and the commercial implications of decisions.

- tag: research-judgment
  shows: Ability to determine what needs researching, which evidence matters, when evidence is sufficient, and when further investigation has low value.

- tag: research-taste
  shows: Ability to identify interesting, relevant, high-signal questions and sources rather than collecting information indiscriminately.

- tag: problem-framing
  shows: Ability to turn an ambiguous or messy situation into a clearly defined problem with useful boundaries and decision criteria.

- tag: product-discovery
  shows: Ability to investigate user needs, constraints, opportunities, assumptions, and possible solutions before committing to implementation.

- tag: prioritization
  shows: Ability to decide what deserves attention first based on impact, uncertainty, effort, risk, dependencies, or strategic value.

- tag: decision-making
  shows: Ability to reach defensible decisions from incomplete information instead of indefinitely gathering more information.

- tag: decision-support
  shows: Ability to structure evidence, alternatives, trade-offs, and recommendations so another person can make a better decision.

- tag: evidence-driven
  shows: Ability to ground conclusions and actions in traceable evidence while clearly separating facts, assumptions, and interpretation.

- tag: research-synthesis
  shows: Ability to combine fragmented information from multiple sources into a concise understanding, conclusion, or actionable recommendation.

- tag: systems-thinking
  shows: Ability to understand interactions, feedback loops, dependencies, second-order effects, and how local changes affect a broader system.

- tag: workflow-automation
  shows: Ability to identify repeatable work and turn it into reliable automated workflows with appropriate controls and exceptions.

- tag: ai-native-workflows
  shows: Ability to redesign work around AI capabilities rather than merely adding AI to an existing manual process.

- tag: human-in-the-loop
  shows: Ability to deliberately place human review, approval, intervention, or judgment where automation should not act autonomously.

- tag: ai-governance
  shows: Ability to define boundaries, controls, permissions, review mechanisms, and accountability for AI-assisted or autonomous systems.

- tag: prototyping
  shows: Ability to rapidly turn an idea or hypothesis into something concrete enough to test, evaluate, communicate, or learn from.

- tag: continuous-improvement
  shows: Ability to create feedback loops where outcomes, failures, and lessons systematically improve future work.

- tag: process-improvement
  shows: Ability to identify friction, waste, failure points, or unnecessary manual work and redesign the process to perform better.

- tag: quality-assurance
  shows: Ability to define checks, validation steps, review mechanisms, and failure detection that improve output reliability.

- tag: quality-gate
  shows: Ability to introduce explicit pass/fail criteria that prevent low-quality or unsafe changes from progressing automatically.

- tag: validation
  shows: Ability to define and execute tests that determine whether assumptions, solutions, or outputs are sufficiently correct to proceed.

- tag: risk-awareness
  shows: Ability to identify meaningful failure modes, unintended consequences, security concerns, operational risks, and appropriate mitigations.

- tag: risk-controls
  shows: Ability to translate identified risks into concrete safeguards, approval requirements, checks, limits, or fallback mechanisms.

- tag: reusable-systems
  shows: Ability to turn one-off solutions and lessons into reusable patterns, infrastructure, templates, instructions, or components.

- tag: reusable-infrastructure
  shows: Ability to create shared technical foundations that reduce repeated implementation work across workflows or projects.

- tag: platform-thinking
  shows: Ability to design capabilities as shared foundations that can support multiple users, workflows, products, or future use cases.

- tag: stakeholder-understanding
  shows: Ability to understand what different users, operators, decision-makers, or stakeholders need from a solution and where their interests differ.

- tag: value-creation
  shows: Ability to connect work to a meaningful improvement for users, operations, the organization, or the business.

- tag: business-value
  shows: Evidence that the work targets or produces a concrete business outcome such as efficiency, revenue, cost reduction, quality, risk reduction, or customer value.

- tag: technical-judgment
  shows: Ability to choose appropriate technical approaches and trade-offs without overengineering or treating technology as the goal itself.

- tag: automation-judgment
  shows: Ability to decide which tasks should be automated, which should remain manual, and what level of autonomy is appropriate.

- tag: context-engineering
  shows: Ability to deliberately structure instructions, memory, retrieved information, tools, and surrounding context so an AI system performs more reliably.

- tag: context-improvement
  shows: Ability to identify weaknesses in the information or instructions available to a system and systematically improve the context it receives.

- tag: decision-traceability
  shows: Ability to preserve the link between evidence, assumptions, decisions, implementation, and later outcomes.

- tag: traceability
  shows: Ability to preserve provenance and relationships between inputs, actions, outputs, authorship, or decisions so work can be inspected later.

- tag: automation-reliability
  shows: Ability to design automated systems that behave predictably despite failures, retries, identity issues, changing inputs, or partial execution.

- tag: recursive-loop
  shows: Ability to design systems where outputs, evaluations, or lessons feed back into subsequent iterations to improve future performance.

- tag: memory-hygiene
  shows: Ability to keep accumulated AI or system memory relevant, structured, current, and free from obsolete or misleading information.

- tag: knowledge-management
  shows: Ability to capture, organize, maintain, and retrieve useful knowledge so it remains usable beyond the original task or contributor.

- tag: organizational-learning
  shows: Ability to convert individual experiences, failures, and discoveries into reusable knowledge that improves future work across a team or system.

- tag: governance-architecture
  shows: Ability to encode responsibilities, permissions, boundaries, and operating rules into the structure of a system rather than relying only on manual discipline.

- tag: design-governance
  shows: Ability to maintain explicit design principles and constraints so implementation remains coherent as a system evolves.

- tag: design-consistency
  shows: Ability to preserve coherent interaction, visual, structural, or architectural patterns across different parts of a product or system.

- tag: design-system
  shows: Ability to establish reusable design rules, components, or conventions that improve consistency and reduce repeated design decisions.

- tag: security-by-design
  shows: Ability to incorporate security considerations into architecture and workflows from the beginning rather than adding controls after implementation.

- tag: least-privilege
  shows: Ability to minimize permissions, credentials, and access scope so systems receive only the authority required to perform their task.
```

## Assignment rules

A tag should answer: **"What capability does this case provide evidence that the person actually demonstrated?"**

1. Assign tags based on observable evidence: decisions, artifacts, behavior, implementation, validation, or measured outcomes.
2. Do not assign a tag merely because related terminology or technology appears in the case.
3. Prefer the most specific recruiter-understandable capability tag.
4. Avoid assigning multiple near-synonyms unless they represent genuinely different evidence.
5. Prefer capability tags over implementation-detail tags.
6. Implementation tags such as `recursive-loop`, `quality-gate`, or `least-privilege` may supplement a broader capability tag but should rarely stand alone.
7. Do not assign outcome tags such as `business-value`, `value-realization`, `adoption`, `impact-measurement`, or `roi-thinking` unless actual evidence exists.
8. Future tags are not aspirational labels. They become valid only when a case demonstrates them.
9. A strong case normally needs only 3-7 tags.
10. When choosing between two similar tags, prefer the one a recruiter or hiring manager can understand without knowing the underlying implementation.
