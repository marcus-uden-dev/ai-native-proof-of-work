# Household Budget App Pricing Strategy

Last updated: 2026-05-11
Status: Deferred hypothesis / Not validated

## Project Scope

Project: household budget app.

Role in portfolio: supporting proof point for financial-product UX, household domain modeling, shared access, and test-backed execution.

## Pricing Philosophy

Pricing should reflect trusted household financial clarity, not generic budgeting charts.

Users would need to trust the product with sensitive financial data before pricing matters.

## Pricing Options Considered

| Model | Pros | Cons | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free local/self-hosted | High trust and low friction | Weak commercial validation | Hypothesis |
| Household subscription | Clear buyer unit | Competitive consumer market | Hypothesis |
| One-time purchase | Simple | Harder to sustain support | Hypothesis |
| Premium forecasting/imports | Ties price to advanced value | Requires strong UX proof | Deferred |

## Current Recommendation

Defer pricing until household workflow safety, import quality, and daily-use value are proven.

## Reasoning Trail

```text
Context → Household finance requires high trust before pricing can be evaluated.
Options Considered → Free/self-hosted, household subscription, one-time purchase, premium imports/forecasting.
Tradeoffs → Consumer finance pricing requires privacy and daily-use trust; early claims would be weak.
Decision → Defer pricing until workflow safety and value are proven.
Evidence → Needs validation.
Open Questions → Which feature creates enough trust and value to justify payment?
Next Action → Validate a privacy-safe sample-data demo path.
```
